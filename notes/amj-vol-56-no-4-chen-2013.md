# Self-Love's Lost Labor: A Self-Enhancement Model of Workplace Incivility

**Abstract**
We present a self-enhancement model of workplace incivility to account for the effects of exposure to incivility on task performance. In particular, we predict that workplace incivility thwarts the ability to self-enhance at work, resulting in employees’ divesting their sense of self from work via disengagement processes. Maintaining high levels of task performance subsequently ceases to be a source of self-enhancement for employees who have disengaged their sense of self from work. We also examined the extent to which the desire for self-enhancement (i.e., narcissism) moderated the effect of incivility on engagement. Using two sets of multiwave, multisource data collected in China, our results provide full support for our hypotheses and provide new theoretical directions for incivility research.

**Research Question**
Does work engagement mediate the negative relationship between workplace incivility and task performance, and does narcissism intensify the incivility–engagement relationship and the indirect relationship with performance?

**Hypotheses / Propositions**
- H1: Workplace incivility has a negative relation with work engagement.
- H2: Work engagement has a positive relation with task performance.
- H3: Work engagement mediates the negative effect of incivility on task performance.
- H4: Narcissism moderates the relation between incivility and work engagement: the negative relation is stronger when narcissism is high.
- H5: Narcissism moderates the negative and indirect effect of workplace incivility on task performance; work engagement mediates the indirect effect when narcissism is high but not when it is low.

**Mechanism Process**
- IV(s): Experienced workplace incivility.
- DV(s): Task performance.
- Mediators: Work engagement.
- Moderators: Narcissism, moderating the incivility–engagement path and thereby the indirect relationship with task performance.

The proposed self-enhancement mechanism is that incivility threatens employees’ positive self-views, prompting them to disengage their sense of self from work for protection. Once the self is less invested in the work role, superior performance provides less opportunity for self-enhancement, weakening motivation to perform well. Narcissistic employees’ particularly strong desire for positive self-views is expected to intensify disengagement following incivility. The studies test associations consistent with this account rather than establishing its causal ordering.

**Data & Measures**
Study 1 surveyed technicians in a large manufacturing company in southern China. From 420 invited subordinates, responses numbered 334 at the first wave and 281 at the second; the final sample contained 235 subordinates with corresponding ratings from supervisors, each of whom rated one subordinate. Three waves were separated by three months, spanning six months: incivility, narcissism, demographics, and controls were assessed first, engagement second, and supervisor-rated performance third.

Study 2 surveyed sales clerks at a property company and a department store in southern China. From 500 invited subordinates, 240 responded initially and 230 at the second wave; the final sample contained 204 subordinates. Its three waves were one month apart, spanning two months. The procedures report 65 supervisors rating the 204 subordinates, whereas Table 7 reports 49 supervisor-level units; the paper does not reconcile these counts. Performance models used supervisor random intercepts; engagement models used hierarchical multiple regression.

Both studies measured past-year incivility with Cortina et al.’s seven-item frequency scale, work engagement with the nine-item Utrecht scale aggregated into one index, and current task performance with a five-item supervisor scale adapted for Chinese contexts. Narcissism used the 16-item NPI in Study 1 and the 40-item NPI in Study 2, both with five-point agreement responses. Study 1 controlled for age, gender, organizational tenure, and conscientiousness. Study 2 controlled for company, age, gender, tenure, rank, the Big Five traits, positive affectivity, interpersonal justice, and self-esteem. Mediation tests included Sobel and bootstrap analyses; Study 2 also used PRODCLIN. The designs are correlational, and calendar dates are not reported.

**Key Findings**
- Study 1 supported H1–H3: incivility negatively predicted engagement (β = −.24, p ≤ .01; Table 2, model 2), engagement positively predicted performance (β = .34, p ≤ .01; Table 3, model 2), and the incivility coefficient became nonsignificant with engagement included (β = −.07; Table 3, model 4). The Sobel test supported mediation (Z = −2.86, p ≤ .01), as did the reported bootstrap interval excluding zero. H4 was supported by the negative interaction (β = −.26, p ≤ .01); incivility–engagement slopes were −.50 at high narcissism (p ≤ .01) and .02 at low narcissism (nonsignificant).
- Study 2 supported H1–H3: incivility negatively predicted engagement (β = −.58, p ≤ .01; Table 6, model 2), engagement positively predicted performance (γ = .44, p ≤ .01; Table 7, model 2), and the incivility coefficient became nonsignificant with engagement included (γ = −.11; Table 7, model 4). The Sobel test supported mediation (Z = −3.81, p ≤ .01), as did reported PRODCLIN and bootstrap intervals excluding zero. H4 was supported (interaction β = −.11, p ≤ .05); incivility–engagement slopes were −.75 at high narcissism and −.48 at low narcissism (both p ≤ .01).
- Moderated path analyses showed stronger negative indirect effects at high narcissism, but qualify the authors’ support claim for H5. Table 4 reports Study 1 indirect effects of −.22 at high narcissism (p ≤ .01) and +.07 at low narcissism (p ≤ .05), with a high-minus-low difference of −.29 (p ≤ .01). Table 8 reports Study 2 indirect effects of −.31 at high and −.18 at low narcissism (both p ≤ .01), with a difference of −.13 (p ≤ .01). Thus, neither table shows the absence of mediation at low narcissism predicted by H5: the low-narcissism indirect effect is positive in Study 1 and negative in Study 2.

**Theoretical Contribution**
The paper integrates self-enhancement and work engagement into incivility theory, linking threats to positive self-views with disengagement and task performance. Combining engagement as a mediator with narcissism as a moderator addresses both how incivility relates to performance and for whom its negative associations are stronger. It also qualifies the idea that positive self-evaluations necessarily facilitate engagement: narcissistic employees can disengage when treatment challenges their self-views.

**Practical Implication**
The authors suggest that researchers examine self-affirmation exercises and mindfulness training as promising ways to mitigate incivility’s effects. Self-affirmation may provide another route to self-enhancement, while mindfulness may reduce excessive attention to the self-relevant and emotional consequences of minor slights. These interventions follow from the theoretical model and were not tested in the two studies.

**Limitations**
Incivility and engagement were self-reported, so common-method variance cannot be conclusively excluded; the correlational design also leaves causal ordering and reverse relationships uncertain. Study 2 controls do not eliminate alternative explanations such as organizational disidentification. Generalizability is uncertain, the studies differ in the low-narcissism engagement relationship, and unmeasured job characteristics may help account for differences between technicians and salespeople.

**Future Research**
The authors call for tests of alternative explanations, including identification processes, and for examining whether the findings hold when job characteristics such as perceived autonomy and task identity are considered. They also propose studying when narcissism might mitigate incivility through ignoring negative feedback or derogating its source; the suggestion that individuals in China’s interdependent cultural context may have been less willing to ignore uncivil treatment is explicitly post hoc. Further self-enhancement research could examine whether incivility prompts greater engagement through reflected glory when the supervisor is a rising star or the company is highly regarded and whether incivility prompts downward social comparisons.
