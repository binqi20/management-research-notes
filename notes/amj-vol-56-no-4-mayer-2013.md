# Integrating Theories in AMJ Articles

**Abstract**
Not reported in paper

**Research Question**
How can researchers integrate theories to generate novel insights into questions that no single theory can adequately address, and what conditions support different approaches to integration?

**Hypotheses / Propositions**
Not reported in paper

**Mechanism Process**
- Single phenomenon, two theoretical perspectives: combine perspectives on a common dependent variable, clarifying each theory's contribution and why neither alone answers the research question. Respect foundational assumptions and resolve disparities relevant to the integration domain. Resource-based and transaction-cost explanations of subcontracting illustrate this approach.
- One phenomenon, seemingly disparate theoretical perspectives: expose complementary assumptions beneath genuine differences between perspectives on the same or highly similar phenomena. Commonality enables productive dialogue without requiring one theory to defeat the other or all tensions to disappear; social-network and leader-member exchange accounts of influence illustrate this approach.
- Applying one theory to the domain of another: articulate a link to the new domain and establish compatible assumptions or overcome incompatibilities. Combining social-psychological theories with transaction cost economics illustrates how contract research can shift from which clauses to include toward how to frame them.
- Streams sharing a similar explanatory account: integrate streams that use common processes or mechanisms to explain distinct phenomena, ideally within a shared domain or around common explanatory variables. The larger framework must have meaning beyond identifying shared foundations; social-exchange explanations of employment relationships illustrate this approach.

The central argument is that integration combines theoretical elements to produce insights unavailable from either theory alone. The research question or phenomenon should guide which elements to retain or modify and how to combine them; merely placing hypotheses from separate theories in one article does not establish integration.

**Theoretical Contribution**
The editorial distinguishes theory integration from the effective but separate use of multiple theories: integration must yield novel insights, either by applying theory to a new domain or enriching understanding within an existing domain. Its four approaches identify different bases for integration, from shared phenomena and complementary assumptions to cross-domain application and common explanatory accounts.

**Practical Implication**
For researchers, the specific research question or phenomenon should filter what to draw from and modify in each theory. Assumption differences can be set aside when irrelevant to the integration domain; when an assumption is critical, authors should explain their position clearly and engage respectfully with the theory.

**Limitations**
The authors do not claim that their four approaches exhaust possible forms of integration. They also identify gaining acceptance from scholars committed to each theory as a challenge, especially when core assumptions are stretched or modified.

**Future Research**
For emergent informal influence, the editorial identifies questions about whether expertise and prototypicality act additively or multiplicatively, including whether prototypicality enhances expertise. It also asks when each matters and whether the nature of the group matters.
