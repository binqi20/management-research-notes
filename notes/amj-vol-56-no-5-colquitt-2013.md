# Crafting References in AMJ Submissions

**Abstract**
Not reported in paper

**Research Question**
How can scholars preparing AMJ submissions craft citations and references that avoid obvious mistakes and exhibit refined craftsmanship?

**Hypotheses / Propositions**
Not reported in paper

**Mechanism Process**
- Basic correctness: Follow AMJ's style guide and proofread references against the original PDF or paper copy, checking inclusion of cited sources, author names, years, volumes, and pages.
- Substantive accuracy: Distinguish theoretical arguments from empirical findings, read original sources rather than copying others' citations, and attach references to the specific clauses they support.
- Seminality: Credit the works that introduced constructs or theories, identify supplementary reviews as reviews, and avoid citing articles that merely refer to earlier empirical work instead of the original evidence.
- Rigor: Give precedence to quantitative studies with strong construct, internal, and statistical conclusion validity, and to qualitative studies that gather and code compelling data using appropriate methodologies to develop rich theoretical insights.
- Source selection: Colquitt regards conference papers as typically less valuable references for empirical findings and favors refereed narrative reviews, conceptual papers, and empirical studies over book chapters covering the same terrain. He excepts book chapters that are seminal or lack a suitable refereed substitute.
- Scope: Balance credit to important work and useful signposts for readers against distraction and unnecessary manuscript length; use "e.g." to indicate nonexhaustive citation strings where appropriate.

Colquitt treats references as an indication of the care invested in constructing a manuscript. Avoiding cosmetic errors removes a distraction but does not itself impress readers or reviewers; refined craftsmanship requires attention to substantive accuracy, seminality, rigor, and scope. These are editorial arguments and recommendations, illustrated through prior literature, rather than relationships tested in an original empirical study.

**Theoretical Contribution**
The editorial organizes advice on citations and references around the distinction between avoiding shoddy craftsmanship and achieving refined craftsmanship. It emphasizes how references connect a manuscript to prior scholarship, acknowledge intellectual indebtedness, and set the stage for theoretical argumentation.

**Practical Implication**
Scholars submitting to AMJ should devote a separate proofread to bibliographic accuracy and read the original sources to ensure that citations faithfully represent their content. Colquitt also urges authors to deliberate over citation choices so that seminal credit, rigor, and appropriate scope receive careful, reasoned consideration.

**Limitations**
Colquitt acknowledges that achieving an appropriate citation scope is often subjective and may conflict with acknowledging seminality. Reasonable scholars can disagree about how much more optimal the scope of his revised illustrative passage is than that of the original.
