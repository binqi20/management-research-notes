# The Last Three Years at AMJ—Celebrating the Big Purple Tent

**Abstract**
Not reported in paper

**Research Question**
How do submissions received and articles published during the outgoing editorial team's three-year term illustrate AMJ's breadth across micro and macro research, theoretical approaches, methods, and international settings?

**Hypotheses / Propositions**
Not reported in paper

**Mechanism Process**
- Micro–macro breadth: The team received 3,358 original submissions, excluding revisions, re-revisions, conditional acceptances, and special research forum submissions; 49% were micro and 51% macro. Separately, it published 174 articles, excluding special research forum articles, with 87 micro and 87 macro articles.
- Routes to theoretical contribution: Around 50% of published articles used existing theory to inspire questions or variables or inform hypotheses or propositions; the remaining 50% focused on their own theorizing through disciplined imagination, induction, or verbal explanations connecting references, data, and diagrams.
- Research diversity: Around 17% of published articles were inductive and qualitative; 12% included a laboratory experiment, compared with 5% in 2008. Around 25% used non-US field samples, drawn from 21 countries across five continents.

The editorial uses these descriptive patterns to portray AMJ as a broad scholarly outlet whose theoretical contribution requirement accommodates different research approaches. Published articles and received submissions represent distinct aspects of the team's term: many published articles had been handled by members of the preceding editorial team.

**Theoretical Contribution**
The editorial presents theoretical contribution as compatible with both established theories and authors' own theorizing. Its account of article openings distinguishes topic-based conversations, theory-based conversations, and phenomenon-driven openings, emphasizing that AMJ values phenomenon-driven work when connected to theoretical understanding.

**Practical Implication**
Authors of phenomenon-driven work are asked to position their phenomenon within an existing theoretical lens or explore it through their own theorizing. The editorial also recalls the team's call for more inductive submissions using more diverse qualitative methods.
