# Organizations with Purpose

**Abstract**
Not reported in paper

**Research Question**
How can rediscovering business purpose—the reason for which a business exists—and connecting it to societal values help organizations generate trust and contribute to the common good?

**Hypotheses / Propositions**
Not reported in paper

**Mechanism Process**
- Common good: Couple business purpose with societal success so that profits derive from products and services that intrinsically benefit society; consider effects on those least able to have a voice.
- Dignity: Respect the whole person and enable people to reach their potential in their various relationships with business.
- Solidarity: Recognize that other people matter, respond to community needs, and include underserved and underprivileged people.
- Plurality: Value diverse thinking and cultures while maintaining shared purpose and values.
- Subsidiarity: Delegate decisions according to the ability to make the right decision, giving employees autonomy, support, voice, and responsibility.
- Reciprocity: Build relationships around honesty, integrity, and mutual benefit, including benefits that individuals and society value but cannot demand.
- Sustainability: Exercise stewardship of people, values, resources, and the environment, accepting responsibility for business impacts and future generations.

The authors present these six values as potential mechanisms through which purpose can build trust and better businesses. Their argument links individual conduct and organizational practices to societal well-being; shareholder wealth and care for others may conflict, but the authors argue that they are not ultimately incompatible in a business whose purpose draws on societal values. Promoting the common good does not mean that every business decision can benefit everyone.

**Theoretical Contribution**
The editorial proposes purpose as an overarching framework connecting research on compassion, meaning at work, social responsibility, justice, value congruence, and diversity to why and for whom businesses exist. It invites scholars to reconsider assumptions about self-interest and to incorporate purpose, care for others, and societal betterment more fully into theories of trust, motivation, and leadership.

**Practical Implication**
Business leaders should make purpose specific enough to guide action, support it with core values, and be willing to be held accountable for reducing harm and enhancing well-being. Employers should respect people as whole persons, while responsible delegation should give employees autonomy, support, and voice to act consistently with purpose. Stewardship involves developing people, nurturing values, preserving and restoring resources, and accepting responsibility for business impacts.

**Future Research**
The authors ask how businesses can sustain purpose and the common good over time, embed them in practices, and determine value beyond financial outcomes. They propose questions about employees bringing their whole selves to work, shared values across cultures, responsible decision making and voice, reciprocal stakeholder relationships, and stewardship of resources and communities. They also call for performance measures encompassing legacy, responsibility, and altruism, and investigation of why businesses began and how some lost their way.
