# Rethinking Governance in Management Research

**Abstract**
Not reported in paper

**Research Question**
How can management scholarship broaden governance research beyond its predominant focus on investor-owned corporations and shareholder–manager incentive alignment to address evolving managerial roles, organizational contexts, processes, and governance forms?

**Hypotheses / Propositions**
Not reported in paper

**Mechanism Process**
- Governance scope: Leadership systems, managerial control protocols, property rights, decision rights, and other practices that give organizations authority and mandates for action.
- Roles and contexts: Managers and their teams in leadership and other roles; private and family firms, entrepreneurial businesses, nongovernmental organizations, and public and private partnerships.
- Processes and change: Internal organizational architecture, coordination, and collaboration; external social processes and policies; temporal effects and governance shifts.

The editorial argues for extending the study of governance beyond shareholder–manager incentive alignment to leadership, coordination, collaboration, and social processes. It connects broader stakeholder participation and interorganizational relationships with questions about legitimate governance claims, coordinated decisions, and the execution of governance duties across organizational boundaries.

**Theoretical Contribution**
The editorial broadens the governance research agenda from investor-owned corporations and discrete governance mechanisms to organizations’ authority, mandates, and relationships with multiple stakeholders. It highlights governance across organizational forms, boundaries, institutional settings, and time, and calls for comparative analysis of the performance and value creation of alternative governance arrangements.

**Practical Implication**
The authors encourage management scholars to consider organizational purpose and the interests of stakeholders beyond firm investors. They also identify a possible practical role for big data in governance through experimentation, structured feedback, and hypothesis-driven inquiry, while emphasizing the need to determine which processes support better decision making.

**Limitations**
Not reported in paper

**Future Research**
Research should examine the boundaries and legitimacy of stakeholder claims, stakeholder participation in decision making and constraints on action, and how CEOs handle multiple, potentially conflicting stakeholder expectations. The authors call for work on which big-data processes improve governance decisions, institutional variation in governance, the distinctive governance challenges of multinational enterprises, and whether governance systems converge or diverge. They also propose examining coordination and conflicts in governance duties across organizational boundaries, the evolution of governance arrangements, and comparative value creation under alternative forms, including corporations, nonprofit organizations, and licensing arrangements.
