# Swift Sense of Community: Resourcing Artifacts for Rapid Community Emergence in a Temporary Organization

**Abstract**
Relational sense of community (SOC) research suggests that SOC depends on the depth of relationships cultivated between members over time. The rise of temporary organizations—transient work arrangements with limited expectations for future interactions— implored us to consider how a swift SOC can emerge in temporary organizations, where the cultivation of relationships may be challenging. We introduce a broader relational approach and draw on high-quality connections and resourcing theories to examine how a swift SOC emerges. Utilizing rich data sources, our qualitative analyses present a swift SOC cultivated in five days in a sleepaway summer camp. We find that this swift SOC was built on brief supportive connections that were made durable by resourcing artifacts. Resourcing artifacts creates scaffolds that mobilize actors to create a web of connections, leading to an organization-wide swift SOC. We propose that a swift SOC can emerge through four intertwined resourcing artifact phases: initial resourcing, embracing resourcing, reinterpreting resourcing, and expanding resourcing. During these phases, individuals imbue artifacts with new meaning and resource artifacts for (a) dyadic connection, (b) staff coordination, and (c) membership in a subgroup and (d) an organization-wide community. We demonstrate symbolizing and momentary connections as novel resourcing mechanisms enabling this process.

**Research Question**
The paper asks how a sense of community can be cultivated in transitory, temporary organizational structures where members' interactions are short-lived. It examines how a swift SOC can emerge when there is low familiarity, limited shared work history, and no expectation of future interactions.

**Hypotheses / Propositions**
No a priori hypotheses. This is an inductive theory-elaboration study, and the authors state their claims as emergent propositions in the theoretical model that follows the findings.
- Overall: a swift SOC can emerge through four intertwined resourcing artifact phases, namely initial resourcing, embracing resourcing, reinterpreting resourcing, and expanding resourcing.
- Phase 1: the emergence of a swift SOC begins with initial resourcing, in which a fleeting experience of positive regard is made durable via staff resourcing of an artifact as an enduring symbol of a dyadic connection, seeding positive regard and mutual fulfillment of needs.
- Phase 2: as competing demands continually recur, staff members engage in embracing resourcing, involving staff adjustments and prescribed dissemination of resourcing artifacts for staff coordination; this expands the scale of resourcing and seeds bottom-up and top-down influence.
- Phase 3: embracing resourcing sparks curiosity in artifact recipients and triggers them to engage in reinterpreting resourcing, which expands the scale and scope of individuals involved, demonstrates lateral (peer-to-peer) influence, and seeds membership and responsibility centered on the well-being of peers in the subgroup.
- Phase 4: symbolizing the artifact with a broad definition of distress turns it into a portal of inclusion; expanding resourcing for an organization-wide community expands the scale and scope of individuals connecting with others and enhances the speed by which they tend to others within and outside the peer subgroup.
- Outcome: at the end of expanding resourcing, when all organizational members are mobilized to forge brief supportive connections, a swift SOC built on the five properties of membership, fulfillment of needs, responsibility for the collective, positive regard, and widespread influence emerges.

**Mechanism Process**
- Key constructs / tensions: Swift sense of community, brief supportive connections, momentary positive regard, widespread influence, resourcing artifacts, and the tension between dyadic care and broader availability.
- Process / phases: Initial resourcing, embracing resourcing, reinterpreting resourcing, and expanding resourcing.
- Practices: Staff and campers imbue bread tags with layered meanings, distribute them, wear them publicly, personalize them, and use them to invite brief supportive connections.
- Emergent outcomes: Positive regard, fulfillment of needs, bottom-up, top-down, and lateral influence, subgroup membership and responsibility, and eventually organization-wide membership and responsibility.
- Boundary conditions: The process is observed in a caring, secluded, five-day temporary organization with high openness to improvisation and broad willingness to disclose distress.

The process begins when a staff member uses a bread tag to make a brief supportive connection with a homesick camper more durable. Staff then embrace the artifact for coordination, using it to signal homesickness and adjust care without repeated probing. Campers reinterpret the artifact as membership in a homesick peer subgroup and later expand it into a symbol of care for many forms of distress across campers and staff. Through these layered acts of symbolizing, the artifact scales dyadic moments into a web of organization-wide supportive connections.

**Data & Measures**
Single case study design (Yin, 1994) of Camp Magic, a seasonal non-profit sleepaway summer camp whose activities are limited to five days within a year. Participant observation was the primary data source, complemented by staff-meeting observations, informal camper interviews, formal staff interviews, pre-camp surveys, and naturalistic observations of pre-camp training.
- Data corpus (Table 1): 50 staff pre-camp surveys and 120 camper pre-camp surveys, both at a 100% response rate; 47 formal staff interviews (94% of staff mapped) lasting 1-1.5 hr (M = 81 min, SD = 11.3 min), of which 42 (89.4%) were conducted at camp during staff break time and 5 in person the week after camp; 73 informal camper interviews (60.08% of campers mapped) lasting 10-45 minutes (M = 25 min, SD = 5.1 min); 50 hours of participant observation by the first author and four research assistants, capturing 10 hours of daily camp activities each day; 11 hours of daily staff-meeting observations (each meeting 1.5-3 hr); and 12 hours of naturalistic observation across all 10 pre-camp staff training sessions (each 1-1.5 hr) held throughout the academic year leading up to camp.
- Coding of focal constructs used a theory elaboration approach (Vaughan, 1992) with SOC, HQCs, and resourcing theory as sensitizing instruments. SOC was coded by combing the data for evidence of its five properties; HQCs were coded as evidence of vitality, positive regard, and mutuality; resourcing was coded as evidence of actions involving artifacts, artifact in use, and reinforced, adjusted, or new schema. Where incoming data conflicted with the sensitizing theory in use, the team added inductive coding, iterating between first-order codes and cycles of deductive reasoning until theoretical saturation (Miles & Huberman, 1994). Coding for shared emotional connection found members' belief that they would continue to share time together was often absent, so first-order codes capturing moments of feeling loved, appreciated, or visible were instead conceptualized as positive regard.
- Analysis proceeded in five stages: a narrative history of the case; selection of theoretical lenses and refinement of the research question; coding to identify the four resourcing phases and their triggers; coding to link phases to outcomes, with member checking against four informants (two former staff members, two former campers); and a reviewer-prompted re-analysis of all staff and camper interview data and meeting transcriptions for hurdles or setbacks.
- Bread tag distribution was counted by source, target, and time over the course of camp (Figure 3). The design is inductive and interpretive; the paper builds a process model and makes no causal-identification claim.

**Key Findings**
Evidence from pre-camp surveys and interviews reflected a limited SOC at the onset of camp, as staff and campers reported they were largely unfamiliar with one another and felt a high degree of social disconnection; within less than five days members reported a noticeable SOC built on organization-wide membership, influence, fulfillment of needs, felt responsibility, and instantaneous experiences of positive regard. The authors find that this swift SOC was built on brief supportive connections made durable by resourcing artifacts, emerging through four intertwined and qualitatively different resourcing phases.
- Phase 1, initial resourcing: a performing paradox, in which a staff member felt torn between one homesick camper's need for her attention and the other 10 campers in her cabin, propelled her to improvise by handing the camper a bread tag to wear on her shoelaces as a symbol of their dyadic connection. This reinforced rather than changed the existing caring staff schema, and seeded positive regard and mutual fulfillment of needs for both parties.
- Phase 2, embracing resourcing: a staff coordination crisis surfaced at the Day 2 staff meeting, together with a staff member sharing the potentiality of her improvised solution, led staff to imbue the tag with an additional meaning as a signal of a specific type of distress (homesickness), and to adjust both their caring schema (getting involved far earlier, withholding inquiry) and their actions. Within three days all staff members embraced resourcing; the paper reports this phase mobilized 48 (96%) staff members to adopt the practice and cultivated an initial connection with 53 homesick campers in three days' time. Outcomes were amplified positive regard and fulfillment of needs, bottom-up influence through staff voice at meetings, and top-down influence once administrators prescribed the practice as a new staff routine.
- Phase 3, reinterpreting resourcing: artifact recipients' curiosity sparked improvised actions, including the shoe kiss invented by two recipients and thereafter used exclusively between those who wore the artifacts, and the tag came to symbolize membership in a peer subgroup the campers called the bread tag secret club. Recipients energized a new schema, reinterpreting their role from recipients of care (87.5% of campers had identified their camp role as recipients of care or support in pre-camp surveys) into caregivers for peers. The phase expanded the scale to 18 campers on Day 3 and 42 campers by Day 4, and produced subgroup membership and responsibility, lateral (peer-to-peer) influence, and amplified positive regard and fulfillment of needs.
- Phase 4, expanding resourcing: recognizing common humanity and feeling doubt about a homesickness-only focus, recipients adjusted their caring schema, secured tags from staff (who initially hesitated, worried the practice would be diluted, but all eventually cooperated), personalized them, and gave them to distressed campers and staff outside the subgroup, including a role reversal in which campers cared for counselors. During this phase 116 individuals, 66 campers and 50 staff members, received a bread tag from artifact recipients, and by Day 5 the paper reports 170 (100%) individuals in different positions of the organization were mobilized to connect with others. Outcomes were organization-wide membership and responsibility, amplified positive regard and fulfillment of needs, and lateral plus bottom-up influence.
- Against the SOC literature, the swift SOC closely echoed three of the five SOC properties (membership, fulfillment of needs, responsibility) but diverged on two: it rested on momentary positive regard rather than a shared emotional connection premised on continuing to share time together, and its influence was widespread rather than only bidirectional, with lateral peer-to-peer influence crucial to emergence. The authors argue bidirectional influence may be sufficient to sustain an existing social order but is necessary yet insufficient for creating a new one.
- A reviewer-prompted search for setbacks found no evidence that those not initially involved challenged the practice, felt offended by it, or attempted to expand the focus of artifact recipients to include them; informants instead pointed to nuances in agreement within the recipient subgroup, which the authors identified as doubt, an additional trigger for expanding resourcing.

**Theoretical Contribution**
The paper extends sense-of-community research by showing that a swift SOC is not merely a faster version of conventional SOC, but a qualitatively different form built on momentary positive regard and widespread influence, including lateral peer influence. It contributes to resourcing theory by identifying symbolizing as a resourcing mechanism through which actors imbue artifacts with layered meanings that can generate both stability and change. It also extends high-quality connections research by explaining how brief dyadic connections can become durable and aggregate into a new social order.

**Practical Implication**
Temporary organizations can cultivate swift community by leaving room for members to improvise, notice distress, and repurpose simple artifacts as visible supports for connection. Leaders should pay attention to organizational openness, because the Camp Magic process depended on staff and campers being able to recognize problems, disclose difficult emotions, and adopt emergent practices quickly. The findings also suggest that community-building in short-lived organizations may depend less on deep prior relationships than on small, emotionally meaningful, and publicly visible moments of support.

**Limitations**
The authors note that Camp Magic is an extreme and caring context, so the process may look different in less care-centered temporary organizations such as movie sets, seasonal festivals, or construction sites. The secluded setting helped reveal the micro-processes of swift SOC emergence, but limited the study's ability to observe spillover effects beyond the camp. The study also focuses on emergence during a five-day period and does not establish whether swift SOC endures after members leave the temporary organization.

**Future Research**
Future research could examine the consequences of swift SOC for relational coordination, voice, word of mouth, and organizational reputation. Researchers could study how vulnerability and present-moment attention enable brief supportive connections, and compare spontaneous versus deliberately engineered swift SOC. Additional work could examine contradictory symbolizing, regeneration of social orders through familiar meanings, spillover beyond the original setting, and whether swift SOC erodes or persists over time.
