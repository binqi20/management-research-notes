# Balancing the Yin and Yang: TMT Gender Diversity, Psychological Safety, and Firm Ambidextrous Strategic Orientation in Chinese High-Tech SMEs

**Abstract**
This paper offers a novel theoretical account of why and when top management team (TMT) gender diversity lends strategic advantage. Building on social role theory, we develop a moderated-mediation model showing: (a) TMT psychological safety mediates the effect of TMT gender diversity on firm ambidextrous strategic orientation (ASO) (why), and (b) firm slack moderates this mediated effect (when). We tested our model in the context of Chinese high-tech smalland medium-sized enterprises. After confirming gender differences in social role–based proclivities at the TMT level, a multiwave survey study of 373 members from 120 TMTs showed that TMT gender diversity positively affects ASO via TMT psychological safety, and this mediated effect is stronger when firm slack is lower than higher.We further interviewed 23 top managers to supplement key quantitative results. Our study advances upper echelons researchon TMTgenderdiversity in two ways. First, ithighlights the gender-specific interpersonal benefit of TMT gender diversity, which is markedly distinct from the cognitive-variety argument associated generically with TMT demographic diversity. Second, it considers both men and women in TMTs in a more balanced manner, thereby offering an alternative account to the female-focused theorization of the positive strategic implications of TMT gender diversity.

**Research Question**
The paper asks why and when TMT gender diversity generates strategic advantage for firms. Specifically, it examines whether TMT psychological safety explains the effect of TMT gender diversity on firm ambidextrous strategic orientation and whether firm slack changes the strength of this mediated relationship.

**Hypotheses / Propositions**
H1. TMT gender diversity is positively related to TMT psychological safety.
H2. TMT psychological safety is positively related to ASO.
H3. TMT psychological safety mediates the positive relationship between TMT gender diversity and firm ambidextrous strategic orientation.
H4. The strength of the mediated effect of TMT gender diversity on firm ambidextrous strategic orientation via TMT psychological safety varies along the degree of firm slack, such that the mediated effect will be stronger when firm slack is lower than higher.

**Mechanism Process**
- IV(s): TMT gender diversity, measured using Blau's index.
- DV(s): Firm ambidextrous strategic orientation, based on exploration and exploitation ratings.
- Mediators: TMT psychological safety.
- Moderators: Firm slack resources, which moderate both the TMT gender diversity-to-psychological safety path and the psychological safety-to-ASO path.

The mechanism is that gender-diverse TMTs combine women's communal, socially sensitive proclivities with men's agentic, voice-raising proclivities. This complementarity fosters psychological safety, understood as both mutual trust and support and the ability to speak up and challenge others. TMT psychological safety then helps members differentiate and integrate exploration and exploitation, supporting ASO. The mediated effect is stronger when firm slack is lower, because resource scarcity makes constructive TMT dynamics more important.

**Data & Measures**
Main study: multiwave survey data from privately held high-tech SMEs in four major cities of China (Beijing, Guangzhou, Shanghai, Chengdu), where firms had fewer than 500 employees and were more than six years old. Research assistants contacted the CEOs of 450 randomly sampled SMEs and 172 agreed to participate; firms with complete responses from at least three TMT members (including CEOs) across all time periods were retained, giving a final sample of 373 TMT members of 120 firms (27% effective response rate; average TMT size 4.38 members; average 71% intrateam response rate; percentage of female members ranged from 0 to 100 and averaged 43%). Surveys were administered at three time periods: demographics, firm slack, and controls at t1; TMT psychological safety two weeks later at t2; ASO about one year later at t3.
- IV — TMT gender diversity: Blau's (1977) index computed over the gender of the CEO and all TMT members, continuous from 0 (same-gender teams) to .50 (gender-balanced teams); the sample spanned that entire feasible range.
- Mediator — TMT psychological safety: Edmondson's (1999) 7-item scale (1 = strongly disagree to 7 = strongly agree; α = .78), aggregated to the team level (mean rwg(j) = .97, ICC[1] = .39, ICC[2] = .66; ANOVA F = 5.49, p < .01).
- DV — ambidextrous strategic orientation: He and Wong's (2004) 8-item scale (1 to 5), with four exploration items (α = .81) and four exploitation items (α = .78); the exploration and exploitation scores were multiplied into a single ASO index, with an additive index used to confirm robustness.
- Moderator — firm slack: Simsek et al.'s (2007) 4-item scale (1 to 5; α = .73), aggregated from TMT members' ratings (mean rwg(j) = .95, ANOVA F = 4.96, p < .01; ICC[1] = .54, ICC[2] = .78).
- Controls: a composite TMT heterogeneity index (education, functional background, firm tenure), TMT tenure diversity, CEO age, CEO-TMT power distance, firm past performance (Ling et al., 2008 scale; α = .95), firm size, firm age, industry sector dummies, and prior ASO measured one year earlier at t1 as a lagged dependent variable.

Analysis: OLS hierarchical regressions test Hypothesis 1 and Hypothesis 2; bias-corrected bootstrapping with 10,000 samples and 95% confidence intervals in the PROCESS macro tests the mediation (Hypothesis 3) and the first-stage, second-stage, and integrated moderated-mediation models (Hypothesis 4). The design is a multiwave survey rather than an experiment; the authors describe temporal separation between waves and the lagged dependent variable as ways to reduce common method bias and endogeneity, and add an instrumental-variable 2SLS check using the existence of women's prior friendship ties and prior colleague ties to other TMT members before joining the TMT as instruments.

Validation study (Appendix A): an online Qualtrics survey sent to top managers of 450 randomly drawn Chinese high-tech SME firms not included in the main-study sample, yielding 219 complete responses (49% response rate; 45% female). Social sensitivity was measured with the "Reading the Mind in the Eyes" test (Baron-Cohen et al., 2001) and voice-raising tendency with a 6-item scale adapted from Van Dyne and LePine (1998) (α = .76), analyzed by MANCOVA and ANCOVA with individual, firm, and industry controls.

Supplementary qualitative component: semistructured interviews of 30-45 minutes each with 23 top managers (11 females and 12 males) of 17 Chinese high-tech SMEs with varied TMT gender diversity, reported as key observations and representative quotes.

**Key Findings**
- Hypothesis 1 supported: TMT gender diversity relates positively to TMT psychological safety (Model 2: B = 0.57, p < .01).
- Hypothesis 2 supported: TMT psychological safety relates positively to ASO (Model 5: B = 3.88, p < .01).
- Hypothesis 3 supported: the indirect effect of TMT gender diversity on ASO via TMT psychological safety is significantly positive (2.20, SE = 0.78, CI [0.87, 3.99]); the direct effect after partialling out the mediator is insignificant (B = 0.72, SE = 1.25, CI [-1.76, 3.21]), while the total effect is significant (B = 2.93, SE = 1.40, CI [0.14, 5.71]).
- Hypothesis 4 supported, first stage: moderated-mediation index = -2.00 (SE = 1.03, CI [-4.34, -0.31]); the indirect effect is significantly positive when slack is low (B = 3.31, SE = 1.09, CI [1.60, 5.97]) and insignificant when slack is high (B = 0.13, SE = 1.03, CI [-1.87, 2.24]); the TMT gender diversity × firm slack interaction is negative (B = -0.61, SE = 0.24, p < .05), and the simple slope of gender diversity on psychological safety is positive and significant at low slack (B = 1.01, SE = 0.26, p < .01) but insignificant at high slack (B = 0.04, SE = 0.28, n.s.).
- Hypothesis 4 supported, second stage: moderated-mediation index = -0.84 (SE = 0.47, CI [-2.13, -0.17]); the indirect effect is strongly positive when slack is low (B = 2.56, SE = 1.08, CI [0.72, 5.07]) and becomes less positive when slack is high (B = 1.21, SE = 0.60, CI [0.33, 2.86]); the TMT psychological safety × firm slack interaction is negative (B = -1.77, SE = 0.67, p < .01), and the positive effect of psychological safety on ASO is stronger at low slack (B = 5.33, SE = 0.82, p < .01) than at high slack (B = 2.52, SE = 0.79, p < .01). In the integrated first- and second-stage model, the indirect effect is significantly positive when slack is low (B = 5.40, SE = 1.63, CI [2.85, 9.35]) but insignificant when slack is high (B = 0.10, SE = 0.80, CI [-1.547, 1.86]).
- Supplemental analyses: with TMT psychological safety and TMT cognitive variety entered as parallel mediators, psychological safety remains a significant mediator (indirect effect = 1.57, SE = 0.62, CI [0.59, 3.13]) while the mediation of cognitive variety is not significant (indirect effect = 0.51, SE = 0.55, CI [-0.44, 1.77]). Using TMT female percentage as an alternative measure, there is no linear effect but a significant inverted-U relationship with TMT psychological safety (turning point at 48%), and an indirect inverted-U relationship with ASO via TMT psychological safety. IV/2SLS checks (IV F = 19.69, p < .01; Sargan χ² = 0.23, p > .05; Durbin-Wu-Hausman F = 3.88, p > .05) indicate that endogeneity did not confound the estimated effect of TMT gender diversity on TMT psychological safety.
- Validation study: women have significantly higher social sensitivity than men (adjusted means 6.04 vs. 4.77; F = 28.21, p < .01, partial η² = .13), whereas men have significantly higher voice-raising tendency than women (adjusted means 5.80 vs. 5.47; F = 16.03, p < .01, partial η² = .08); the MANCOVA on both proclivities as a set is significant (Wilks' λ = .76, F = 29.55, p < .01).
- Interviews: female executives tended to be more socially sensitive and male executives more inclined to raise their voice; gender-diverse TMTs tended to experience considerable psychological safety, whereas female-majority TMTs were more prone to seeking cohesion and male-majority TMTs showed more conflicts and confrontations.

**Theoretical Contribution**
The paper challenges the generic cognitive-variety explanation for TMT demographic diversity by arguing that the strategic value of TMT gender diversity is interpersonal and gender-specific. It contributes to upper echelons research by showing that TMT psychological safety transmits the relationship between gender diversity and ASO. It also shifts theorizing away from a female-centered "more women is better" account toward a gender-balance account that treats men and women as complementary participants in TMT dynamics.

**Practical Implication**
The paper offers no practical-implications section. Its findings indicate that TMT gender diversity positively affects firm ambidextrous strategic orientation via TMT psychological safety, so the channel is the interpersonal quality of the team's strategic decision-making — whether members can support one another and openly express their own proclivities and interests — rather than the cognitive variety usually attributed to demographic diversity. The paper also reports that this mediated effect is stronger when firm slack is lower than higher.

**Limitations**
The authors state that the SME setting may limit generalizability to large and established firms. They also note that the Chinese context may limit generalizability to other countries, including countries with different levels of gender egalitarianism. The study focuses on ASO and firm slack, leaving other strategic outcomes and other external environmental moderators for future work.

**Future Research**
Future research could test the interpersonal benefits of TMT gender balance in large firms and in countries with different cultural gender norms. Studies could examine other outcomes shaped by TMT psychological safety, such as entrepreneurial orientation or other strategic orientations. Additional work could also test whether munificence, dynamism, uncertainty, or other external conditions moderate the mediated effect of TMT gender diversity.
