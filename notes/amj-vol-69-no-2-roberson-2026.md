# Artificial Intelligence and Responsible Research at AMJ

**Abstract**
Not reported in paper

**Research Question**
How can new AI tools be integrated into research while preserving the integrity of scholarly contributions, and what does responsible use imply for AMJ authors and reviewers?

**Hypotheses / Propositions**
Not reported in paper

**Mechanism Process**
- Author accountability: AI may assist research, but responsibility for the accuracy, interpretation, originality, and theoretical contribution of the work remains with scholars; AI systems cannot be authors.
- Transparency and verification: authors explain which tools were used and for what purposes, document research procedures, and verify AI-assisted outputs.
- Confidentiality and scholarly judgment: reviewers protect unpublished work and evaluate manuscripts through their own expertise rather than delegating evaluation to AI.

The editorial argues that technological assistance does not transfer intellectual responsibility from scholars to tools. Transparent descriptions of research processes allow the editorial team to assess the credibility of evidence and the strength of theoretical claims. AI outputs can contain inaccuracies, and uploading unpublished work to external systems can create privacy risks, making critical verification and protection of confidential material integral to responsible use.

**Theoretical Contribution**
The editorial argues that emerging norms for AI use reaffirm existing standards of authorship, transparency, and accountability rather than introduce entirely new scholarly standards. It connects responsible AI use to preserving scholars' responsibility for theoretical insight and AMJ's mission of advancing theory through rigorous empirical research.

**Practical Implication**
AMJ authors should protect sensitive and unpublished data, retain responsibility for intellectual contributions without listing AI as an author, verify AI-assisted citations, summaries, and analyses, and disclose the tool and purpose in the cover letter, submission checklist, and acknowledgments, adding detail in methods or supplementary materials when relevant. AMJ reviewers should keep manuscripts, data, and review materials out of AI systems, evaluate submissions independently without using AI to review or evaluate manuscripts or to generate, draft, or assist in writing reviews, and request clearer methods and verified sources where needed. They may also encourage authors to disclose AI use when it appears material to manuscript development. Table 1 separately states that reviewers who use AI for limited purposes such as language refinement should disclose this use to the editor.
