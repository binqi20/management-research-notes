# Loosening the Theoretical Reins: A Case for Phenomenon-Driven Research in AMJ

**Abstract**
Not reported in paper

**Research Question**
How can phenomenon-driven research promote theoretical development in management, and how can AMJ authors, reviewers, and editors motivate, develop, and evaluate such work when its theoretical contribution is not immediately apparent?

**Hypotheses / Propositions**
Not reported in paper

**Mechanism Process**
- Core argument: Important phenomena that are neglected, poorly understood, or puzzling can motivate theoretical renewal.
- Theory lifecycle: The authors describe generative emergence, productive application, institutionalization, and possible ossification, followed by opportunities for phenomenon-driven renewal.
- Theories and phenomena: Theoretical traditions develop as vertical columns; phenomena run horizontally across or beyond them, requiring new explanations or integration across traditions.
- Theoretical agnosticism: Suspend early commitment to a particular theory while retaining engagement with prior scholarship.

For qualitative work, the authors describe overlapping, iterative moves of pattern recognition, evaluating candidate explanations, cycling between data and explanation, conceptual construction, and propositional development. For quantitative work, opportunity recognition informs research questions and conceptual construction, followed by candidate explanations and ex-ante hypotheses that guide research designs capable of distinguishing plausible alternatives. Theoretical agnosticism requires transparent, principled exploration and substantive explanation; it does not license HARKing.

**Theoretical Contribution**
The editorial explains how phenomenon-driven inquiry can renew theory by developing new conceptual terrain and connecting existing traditions. Drawing on the builder, expander, and tester categories of Colquitt and Zapata-Phelan, it identifies pathways through new vocabulary or previously unspecified mechanisms for builders, and boundary identification, explanation of established relationships, or cross-tradition integration for expanders. Testers can contribute through demanding tests of existing theory in contexts that reveal its conditions of applicability; phenomenon-driven motivation does not remove the requirement for a theoretical contribution.

**Practical Implication**
AMJ authors can motivate research through neglected, poorly understood, or puzzling phenomena while identifying the scholarly conversation and explaining what the field already knows. Reviewers and editors are encouraged to assess the phenomenon and empirical grounding before demanding a fully crystallized contribution, calibrate feedback to the intended contribution, and examine how explanations were developed. Developmental review should question preferences for familiar theoretical frames and allow contributions to emerge across rounds while retaining standards of rigor and contribution.

**Limitations**
The proposed motivations and contribution pathways are illustrative rather than exhaustive, and the guiding principles encourage a flexible, generative mindset rather than prescribe a template. New conceptual vocabulary requires justification: researchers must assess whether existing concepts, properly applied, already explain the phenomenon.

**Future Research**
The authors call for greater attention to emerging or poorly understood organizational phenomena that expose the limits of existing theory. They highlight AI in organizational work, climate adaptation, platform-based economies, and blurred work–nonwork boundaries among the phenomena demanding scholarly attention.
